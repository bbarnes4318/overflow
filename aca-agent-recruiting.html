01 — The division of labour

You run the operation. We build the roster.

An ACA agency in season needs producers who are licensed, marketplace-registered, and ready to write on day one. Finding them is a full-time job in the weeks you can least afford one.

Recruiting is outreach at scale, then verification, then scheduling. NetEnroll runs its own outbound messaging platform, so we reach producers who are already selling — between agencies, unhappy with a split, or writing other lines and wanting marketplace business this season — instead of waiting for whoever answers a job board.

Every producer is checked before you hear their name: resident license and health line of authority, the non-resident licenses you need, the NPN matched to the license record, marketplace registration for the plan year, and the carrier appointments they already hold. Then they are put on your calendar with that profile in writing.

What happens after the interview is yours. The producer signs your contract, writes under your agency, and keeps the business. NetEnroll takes no piece of it. Our only measure is whether they are still with you thirty days on — and if not, we replace them.

04 — Who this is for

This isn't for every agency.

Producers stay where the machine already exists. Our thirty-day guarantee only makes sense with agencies that have one. Before you fund a campaign, we agree on four things.

A fit looks like this

  • ✓Contracts ready. A contracting workflow that can process carrier appointments promptly once a producer signs.
  • ✓Interviews inside two business days. A verified producer who waits a week takes another offer. Speed is the whole game.
  • ✓Live lead flow, calls or a book. Something for a new producer to write in the first week, not the first month.
  • ✓Prompt status on offers. Tell us who you signed and who you passed on, so the pipeline stays aligned and the count stays honest.

Probably not a fit

  • —You want resumes to sort through, rather than verified producers scheduled to talk.
  • —Producers would be starting from zero: no leads, no calls, no training on your products and tools.
  • —A recruiting budget that has to be paid on the back end from what the producer writes.
  • —A count under five. The minimum campaign is five producers.

What producers ask for is what keeps them: lead flow, working technology, practical training, a clean path from conversation to submitted application, and someone to call when it goes wrong. If your agency has that, they stay and the guarantee costs us nothing. If it doesn't, no recruiter can fix it.
